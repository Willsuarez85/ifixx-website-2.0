**Gray boards, a finish that peels, wood that splinters under bare feet.** Often the wood underneath is still sound, and the job is surface prep and a new finish. When it is not, the bad boards get repaired first.

Whether you call it deck staining, sealing, refinishing, or restoration, the job has the same three parts: fix what is broken, prep the surface, and put the right finish on. We do it on decks and porch floors in [South Charlotte](/service-areas/south-charlotte), Ballantyne, Matthews, Mint Hill, Pineville, Waxhaw, Weddington, Fort Mill, and Indian Land.

## Repair before stain

A new finish over a soft board or a loose railing only hides the problem for a while, and you end up paying for the finish twice. So when the photos or the visit show wood that needs work, we price the repair first and do it first, the same way we handle any [deck and porch repair](/repairs/deck-repair). New boards and weathered boards take a finish differently, and we tell you what to expect before anything goes on.

## Stain, seal, or paint: the honest version

- **Clear sealer** keeps the natural look of the wood. It shows the grain and helps keep water from soaking in, but it adds little or no color.
- **Stain** adds color. A semi-transparent stain lets the grain show through. A solid stain covers more of it and can even out boards that do not match.
- **Paint** covers the wood completely. It fits porch floors and decks that already carry paint or a solid finish. Once a deck is painted, going back to a natural look means stripping it, so we only suggest paint when it is the right finish for that deck.

Which one lasts longer on your deck depends on how much sun and foot traffic it gets, what is on the wood now, and the shape the boards are in. We tell you what we would use and why, and the estimate confirms it.

## How the job goes

Surface prep is part of the staining job, not an extra: the loose finish comes off and the boards get sanded so the new finish can soak in or grip. Then the finish goes on the deck, the railings, and the steps. We walk the deck with you at the end, and you get the price before any work starts.

## Porch floors and steps too

Porch floors and porch steps get the same treatment: repairs first, surface prep, then stain, sealer, or paint. A porch floor that already has paint on it usually stays painted, because stain cannot soak into wood that is sealed under paint.
