Nationally, a kitchen remodel takes about 6 to 12 weeks of work when the layout stays the same, and a bathroom remodel about 2 to 4 weeks, depending on the scope. Move walls, the sink or the shower, and the whole project can run several months.

The work in your home is only part of the calendar. The rest is the wait for cabinets, stone and tile, and that wait is set by the decisions you make before anyone picks up a hammer.

That's why this winter matters in Charlotte. Decide in November and December, order before the holidays, and you can start in January or February with everything on site, ahead of the spring rush.

These are national ranges. Where your project lands depends on the scope, the materials you pick and what's behind the walls.

The planning side is longer than most people expect. ### Where the weeks go in a bathroom

![Primary bathroom remodel in progress, with two new sage green vanities and quartz tops installed, hardware still going on and floor protection down in the next room](/images/projects/bathroom-remodeling-dual-vanity-installation-progress.webp)

A moderate bathroom moves in short steps, and each one waits for the last. Kitchen Cabinet Kings breaks it down like this, assuming nothing is backordered:

- Demolition: 1 to 2 days
- Rough-in for framing, pipes, wiring and ventilation: 2 to 4 days, then an inspection when one applies
- Drywall: 1 to 2 days
- Tile: 3 to 7 days
- Vanity, sink, faucet and hardware: 1 to 2 days
- Fixture hookups, final inspection and finishing touches: 3 to 5 days

Tile is the longest step, and the waterproofing under it needs its cure time. This is the part we don't cut corners on.

That's also why a tiled shower isn't a one-day job. The one-day systems use acrylic wall panels and a molded base instead of tile. If you're weighing the two, our [tub to shower conversion guide](/blog/tub-to-shower-conversion-charlotte) compares them.

## What to decide first

A remodel is won or lost before demo day. Make these five decisions in this order and the calendar gets shorter on its own.

### 1. Your budget ceiling and your cushion

Set the top number first and hold back a cushion for what shows up behind the walls. Every other choice gets measured against that number.

For real Charlotte ranges, see our [kitchen remodel cost guide](/blog/how-much-kitchen-remodel-charlotte) and our [bathroom remodel cost guide](/blog/how-much-bathroom-remodel-charlotte). This article sticks to time.

### 2. Whether the layout stays

This is the decision that changes the clock the most. A same-layout kitchen runs 6 to 12 weeks of work. Moving things pushes it toward 3 to 8 months from start to finish, per Fixr.

Keeping the sink, the range and the shower where they are also keeps the pipes and the wiring where they are. That usually means fewer trades, fewer inspections and fewer surprises.

If you're going to save somewhere, save here. The money goes into what you see and touch every day.

### 3. The slow materials

Order the pieces with the longest wait first:

- **Cabinets or vanity.** The slowest piece. Stock cabinets take 1 to 2 weeks, semi-custom 4 to 8 and fully custom 3 to 6 months or longer, per Fixr.
- **Appliances.** Pick models early so the cabinets are built around their real sizes.
- **Countertops.** Stone is measured on the installed cabinets. Plan about 10 to 14 days from templating to installation, and 3 to 4 weeks in busy months, per [Slabwise](https://slabwise.com/guides/how-long-does-it-take-to-install-countertops).
- Our [countertop guide](/blog/best-kitchen-countertop-materials-for-the-money) explains that sequence and which stone gives you the most for the money.
- **Tub, shower base, tile and glass.** Have them on site before demolition. Shower glass is measured after the tile is up, so it always comes near the end.

Semi-custom or stock cabinets with a solid box and good hinges and drawer slides are where the value is. Ask what the box is made of before you ask what the doors look like.

### 4. Where you'll cook or shower in the meantime

Pick the room for a temporary kitchen: the fridge, a microwave, a slow cooker, a coffee maker and a folding table. Plan paper plates and a few meals out for the weeks without a sink.

For a bathroom, check that a second shower works well before demo day. If it's the only one in the house, the schedule needs to keep the shower down as few days as possible.

### 5. The start date

Pick it last, once the slow materials have a delivery date. We like to schedule demolition once the cabinets or the vanity have a confirmed delivery date, so your kitchen isn't open while you wait on a truck.

## Why winter is the moment to plan a remodel in Charlotte

Indoor work doesn't stop for the cold, and winter has three things going for it.

### You get ahead of the January wave

Remodel searches across the US bottom out in November and December and jump in January. In Charlotte the curve is gentler, but searches for kitchen remodeling still climb from November into January.

If you decide now, your order goes in before that crowd places theirs.

### The holidays slow the factories, so order before them

Many cabinet makers close for one to two weeks between Christmas and New Year's, so an order placed in mid-December may not start until January, per [Kitchen Cabinet Kings](https://kitchencabinetkings.com/blog/kitchen-cabinet-lead-times/).

The same source says spring is the busiest season, and an April cabinet order can take one to two weeks longer than the same order placed in January.

### Imported cabinets and vanities are scheduled for a higher tariff on January 1, 2027

Imported kitchen cabinets and vanities carry a 25% tariff today under the federal Section 232 wood products tariff.

The increase to 50% is scheduled for January 1, 2027, with exceptions for countries that have a trade agreement, according to [STR Trade](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/str-trade-report/trade-report/january/tariff-increase-delayed-on-furniture-kitchen-cabinets-vanities).

How much of that reaches your price depends on where your cabinets are made and what the supplier does, so ask before you order.

Residential building materials are rising too: up 5.5% over the year through September 2026, the highest rate since December 2022, per [NAHB](https://eyeonhousing.org/2026/10/building-material-price-inflation-nears-four-year-high/).

### A winter calendar for a spring kitchen

| Month | What happens |
|---|---|
| November | Budget, scope and layout decided. In-person estimate and walkthrough |
| December | Cabinets, vanity, appliances and tile ordered before the holiday closures |
| January | Stock materials on site; semi-custom arriving. Demolition and rough-in once they're in |

A same-layout bathroom fits inside January or February. A kitchen with a layout change needs more room on the calendar, so start the planning now.

## Do you need a permit? North Carolina's $40,000 rule

North Carolina law, [G.S. 160D-1110(c)](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_160D/GS_160D-1110.html), exempts many projects of $40,000 or less in a single-family home from a building permit.

The exemption doesn't apply when the work adds, repairs or replaces load-bearing structure, adds or changes the design of the pipes, or adds, replaces or changes the design of the wiring, wired-in appliances and equipment, or the heating and cooling system. Swapping a light fixture or an outlet for a like-kind one stays exempt.

In practice, a kitchen or bath that keeps the pipes and the wiring where they are, swaps fixtures like for like and stays under that number may not need a building permit.

Moving the sink, the drain or the shower, adding circuits or replacing wired-in appliances can bring a permit and an inspection, and we coordinate both with the trade that does that work.

Each inspection adds days to the calendar, so it belongs in the schedule from day one. Check your exact project with Mecklenburg County, or with Union County if you live there (Waxhaw and Monroe have their own permit offices).

## Mistakes that make a remodel drag on

- **Changing your mind with the room open.** A new tile or a moved outlet after demolition can stop the crew for days while materials and trades get rescheduled.
- **Ordering the slow parts after demo day.** If the cabinets arrive six weeks after the old ones come out, you live six extra weeks without a kitchen.
- **Buying before measuring.** A vanity or range that doesn't fit the real space is a return, a reorder and a new wait.
- **Templating too early.** Stone is measured on cabinets that are set, level and secured. Measure before that and the countertop won't fit.
- **No cushion for what's behind the walls.** Mold found during demolition can add about a week to a kitchen, per Fixr.
- **Skipping the permit.** An inspection that wasn't scheduled can reopen a finished wall.

We'd rather tell you now than on demo day: most delays begin with a decision that wasn't made yet.
