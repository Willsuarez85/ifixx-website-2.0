Getting a fence quote in Charlotte and wondering whether the number is fair? Most homeowners want the same thing before they call anyone: **a realistic range for their yard.**

**Short answer:** a typical Charlotte backyard fence runs about 150 linear feet and costs **$2,700-$11,250 installed** in 2026 for the fence run itself, before gates ($250-$650 per walk gate, more for a drive gate) and hauling away an old fence. Pressure-treated wood privacy fencing, the most common choice here, lands at **$25-$45 per linear foot** ($3,750-$6,750 for that 150-foot yard). Vinyl runs $40-$70 per foot and aluminum $40-$75. Gates, slope, and tear-out are the line items that move the total most.

Below is the full breakdown: what each material costs, what pushes a quote up, and when a repair makes more sense than a replacement.

## What Drives Fence Costs in Charlotte?

### 1. Material (the biggest lever)

- **Pressure-treated pine** is the local budget workhorse: lowest upfront cost, full privacy, and it handles Carolina humidity when you stain and seal it every 2-3 years.
- **Cedar** resists rot naturally and holds its look longer than pine, at a step up in price.
- **Vinyl** costs more upfront and then asks almost nothing of you: no staining, no sealing, no rotted pickets.
- **Aluminum** is about defining the yard and meeting pool codes, not full privacy. Low maintenance, clean look, priced with vinyl.
- **Chain link** is the cheapest way to enclose a yard for pets or kids when privacy is not the goal.

**Pro tip:** wood is cheaper to install; vinyl and aluminum are often cheaper over 12-15 years once you count staining, sealing, and replacing warped boards. Choose by how long you plan to stay, not just the install price.

### 2. Length and Height

Fencing is priced by the foot, so the property line does most of the math. Two things change the per-foot number:

- **Height.** Going from a 6-foot privacy fence to 8 feet adds roughly 20-35% per foot in material and labor.
- **Partial vs full enclosure.** Fencing the back portion of the yard instead of the full property line is a common way to take 30-50% off a quote without giving up a usable backyard.

### 3. Gates

Gates are the single most underestimated line item on a fence quote:

- **Standard walk gate:** $250 - $650 each
- **Double or drive gate:** $700 - $1,800

Gate hardware is also where a cheap fence shows its age first. A gate that drags after one season is almost always a hardware and post-setting problem, not a material problem.

### 4. Terrain and the Property Line

- **Slope.** Stepped or racked panels on a sloped run take more cuts and more labor. Common on South Charlotte and Waxhaw lots.
- **Rocky or root-bound ground.** Post holes are the slow part of any fence, and hard ground slows them down further.
- **Tear-out and haul-away.** Removing an old fence runs **$3-$8 per linear foot** on top of the new install.
- **Survey and setback.** If the line is unclear, a survey protects you from a neighbor dispute later. That's money well spent before posts go in, not after.

### 5. Permits and HOA Rules

Mecklenburg and Union County both have rules on fence height and placement, and some fences need a permit: typically anything over 6 feet, or a fence tied to a pool enclosure. Many South Charlotte, Ballantyne, Waxhaw, and Weddington neighborhoods add HOA requirements on style, color, and which side of the panel faces out. Approval takes time, so start it before you schedule the install. We will tell you honestly what your project is likely to require so you can plan for it.

## Fence Repair vs. Replacement

Not every tired fence needs to come down. Common Charlotte fence repairs:

| Repair | Typical Cost |
|--------|-------------|
| Reset or replace a leaning post | $150 - $450 per post |
| Replace a fence section or panel | $150 - $400 per section |
| Re-hang or rebuild a sagging gate | $150 - $500 |
| Storm damage, a few sections down | $400 - $1,200 |
| Replace rotted rails and pickets along a run | $8 - $20 per linear foot |

The rule of thumb: if the posts are solid and the damage is limited to panels, rails, or a gate, repair wins on cost every time. Once posts are rotting at the ground line along most of the run, you're paying replacement labor to save recycled material, and a new fence is the better buy. We [repair fences as well as install them](/fence-repair-south-charlotte), so we have no reason to push you toward the expensive answer.

## How to Budget Your Fence Project

1. **Measure the run.** Walk the property line with a tape or a measuring wheel and write down the total feet. That single number drives 80% of the quote.
2. **Count your gates before you compare quotes.** Two walk gates and a drive gate can be $2,000 of the difference between two bids.
3. **Pick material by maintenance tolerance.** Wood is cheapest today, vinyl and aluminum are cheapest over 12-15 years.
4. **Get tear-out, haul-away, and gate hardware in writing.** These are the most common surprise costs on a fence replacement.
5. **Start HOA approval early.** It's usually the longest lead time in the project, so it should be the first thing you set in motion, not the last.

## Frequently Asked Questions - Fence Cost Charlotte NC

**How much does a fence cost in Charlotte NC?**
A typical 150-foot backyard fence in Charlotte costs $2,700-$11,250 installed in 2026, before gates and old-fence removal. Pressure-treated wood privacy fencing runs $25-$45 per linear foot, cedar $35-$60, vinyl $40-$70, aluminum $40-$75, and chain link $18-$35. Budget $250-$650 for each walk gate and $3-$8 per linear foot to haul away an existing fence.

**How much does a 6-foot privacy fence cost per foot in Charlotte?**
A 6-foot pressure-treated wood privacy fence costs $25-$45 per linear foot installed in the Charlotte area in 2026. The same fence in vinyl runs $40-$70 per foot. Going to 8 feet tall adds roughly 20-35% per foot, and it also crosses the height that typically requires a permit.

**Is vinyl fencing worth it compared to wood?**
It depends on how long you plan to stay. Vinyl costs about 60% more to install but needs no staining or sealing and doesn't rot, so it usually costs less over 12-15 years. Wood is the better value if you plan to move within a few years or you want the lowest upfront number.

**Do I need a permit or HOA approval for a fence in Charlotte or Waxhaw?**
Often, yes, and most often for the HOA approval. Some fences need a permit too, typically anything over 6 feet tall. Mecklenburg and Union County both regulate fence height and placement, and many South Charlotte, Ballantyne, Waxhaw, and Weddington neighborhoods require HOA approval of style, height, and color before installation. Approval time is often the longest lead time in the project, so start it early.

**Is it cheaper to repair or replace a fence?**
If the posts are solid and the damage is limited to panels, rails, or a gate, repair is far cheaper: most post, panel, and gate repairs run $150-$1,200, and replacing rotted rails and pickets along a run adds $8-$20 per linear foot. Once posts are rotting at the ground line along most of the run, replacement costs less per year of fence life.

**How long does a fence take to install?**
A straightforward residential run is often a day or two once materials are in and approvals are done. Longer runs, sloped or rocky ground, and multiple gates take longer. We give you a realistic timeline with your quote.
