The best kitchen countertop materials for the money are tier 1 granite, entry-level quartz, modern laminate, butcher block and solid surface. For most full kitchen remodels in 2027, tier 1 granite gives you the most per dollar, and the new 25% tariff on imported quartz widens that lead.

We remodel kitchens. We don't sell stone and we don't run a showroom, so we look at the countertop as one piece of the whole job: the cabinets under it, the sink in it and the schedule around it.

Below you'll find installed prices with their sources, quartz vs granite after the tariff, a 40 square foot budget, 8 ways to save and the few places where saving costs you later.

## The short answer: the best countertop for the money

| Material | Installed price per sq ft | Lifespan | Upkeep | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 granite** | $40 to $80 | 30+ years | Seal every 1 to 3 years | Cooks who want natural stone at the lowest price |
| **Entry-level quartz** | $45 to $85 | 25+ years | No sealing; trivets for hot pans | Low upkeep and an even color |
| **Modern laminate** | $15 to $50 | 10 to 15 years | Low, but damage usually means replacing it | Refreshes, rentals, the tightest budgets |
| **Butcher block** | $55 to $110 | 20+ years with care | Oil every few months; keep water off | Islands and work zones |
| **Solid surface** | $50 to $100 | 20 to 30 years | Light scratches and scorch marks sand out | Long runs with near-invisible seams |

Prices come from [Block Renovation's July 2026 guide to affordable countertops](https://www.blockrenovation.com/guides/cheapest-countertop-materials-that-still-last).

[Fixr's 2026 countertop cost data](https://www.fixr.com/costs/install-kitchen-countertop) shows wider ranges, granite up to $150 and quartz past $200, because it includes premium slabs.

Our take: tier 1 granite for most full kitchens. Quartz if you never want to think about sealing. Laminate when the budget is the deciding factor and the kitchen needs to look clean for the next decade.

## The 5 best kitchen countertop materials, with honest pros and cons

### 1. Tier 1 granite

Granite is sold in tiers based on how common the color is. Tier 1 covers the everyday grays, blacks and speckled beiges you see in a lot of Charlotte kitchens. It performs the same in a kitchen as tier 3, at a much lower price.

**Pros**
- Handles hot pans better than anything else on this list.
- Lasts 30 years or more, and every slab is one of a kind.
- Natural stone was excluded from the 2026 quartz tariff.

**Cons**
- Needs sealing every 1 to 3 years. A bottle of penetrating sealer is cheap and the job takes about 20 minutes.
- In Consumer Reports testing, granite edges and corners were more prone to chipping than quartz.
- The pattern varies across a slab, so look at the full slab before anyone cuts it.

### 2. Entry-level quartz

Quartz is engineered stone: ground quartz bound with resin and pressed into slabs. Entry-level colors are the solids and light patterns, and they price close to tier 1 granite.

**Pros**
- Came out as the toughest surface in Consumer Reports testing.
- Never needs sealing. Wipe it and you're done.
- Even color from one slab to the next, so what you pick is what you get.

**Cons**
- Heat. Quartz can scorch from around 300°F, so hot pans and slow cookers go on a trivet.
- Most imported quartz has paid a 25% tariff since August 15, 2026.
- California has moved toward restricting engineered stone fabrication after silicosis cases among the workers who cut it. That risk comes from the dust when slabs are cut in the shop.

### 3. Modern laminate

Newer laminates print large stone patterns and come with squared edges. From a few feet away, many pass for stone at a fraction of the price.

**Pros**
- The lowest price on the list, $15 to $50 per square foot installed.
- Light enough for any cabinets, with no reinforcement needed.
- A solid choice for a rental, a quick refresh or a kitchen you plan to redo later.

**Cons**
- Knives and hot pans damage it, and Consumer Reports testing confirmed it.
- A burn or a deep cut usually means replacing that section.
- Lasts about 10 to 15 years, and undermount sinks are usually off the table.

### 4. Butcher block

Wood counters bring warmth that stone can't, and they work best on an island or a baking station. Installed prices run $55 to $110 per square foot, while DIY-store sections start around $150 each, per Block Renovation.

**Pros**
- Can be sanded and refinished when it gets marked up.
- Easy on your knives.
- Pairs well with stone on the perimeter.

**Cons**
- Needs oil every few months.
- Standing water is its enemy, so think twice about it right around the sink.
- Dents and knife marks are part of the deal.

### 5. Solid surface

Solid surface is a resin-based material that can be joined with nearly invisible seams, and it can even have the sink molded in. It costs about the same as tier 1 granite.

**Pros**
- Seams you can barely see, great for long runs.
- Light scratches and small scorch marks sand out.
- Lasts 20 to 30 years.

**Cons**
- Softer than stone, so it scratches more easily.
- Hot pans can damage it. Trivets again.
- At this price, many people choose granite for the same money.

### What didn't make the list

Quartzite, marble and porcelain slabs all look great. Quartzite and marble cost more, and marble stains and etches easily. Porcelain is tough once installed but delicate to fabricate, and fewer shops do it well. None of them is the best value per dollar.

## Quartz vs granite: which is cheaper and which lasts longer

| | Tier 1 granite | Entry-level quartz |
|---|---|---|
| **Installed price** | $40 to $80 per sq ft | $45 to $85 per sq ft (before the tariff) |
| **Heat** | Handles hot pans | Can scorch around 300°F |
| **Stains** | Resists them when sealed | Resists them with no sealing |
| **Chips** | Edges and corners can chip | Toughest in testing |
| **Upkeep** | Seal every 1 to 3 years | Wipe and go |
| **2026 tariff** | Excluded | 25% on imports since Aug 15, 2026 |

**Which is cheaper?** On paper the two overlap. Since August 15, 2026, most imported quartz pays a 25% tariff in the first year, or 50% above the import quota. The measure runs four years and steps down slightly each year, according to [the cabinet industry association KCMA](https://kcma.org/insights/quartz-surface-products-201-safeguard-determination).

Granite and other natural stone are excluded. The quartz prices in the table were published a month before the tariff started, so quartz quotes may come in higher than those numbers. How much of the tariff reaches your quote depends on the slab and when your fabricator bought it. Ask.

**Which lasts longer?** Both last decades. Granite takes heat better, and quartz takes stains better without any sealing. If you cook hot and fast, go with granite. If you'd rather never think about upkeep, go with quartz.

## How much to budget for countertops: a 40 sq ft kitchen

Many kitchens land between 35 and 45 square feet of countertop. Here's what 40 square feet costs at the installed prices above.

| Material | 40 sq ft installed |
|---|---|
| Tier 1 granite | $1,600 to $3,200 |
| Entry-level quartz | $1,800 to $3,400 |
| Modern laminate | $600 to $2,000 |
| Butcher block | $2,200 to $4,400 |
| Solid surface | $2,000 to $4,000 |

The countertop price is only part of the quote. Here's what else usually shows up, with prices from Fixr unless noted:

- **Sink and cooktop cutouts:** $100 to $200 each.
- **Edge upgrades:** eased or square edges are usually included. Bevel or bullnose runs $10 to $15 per linear foot, and ogee, mitered or dupont $20 to $40.
- **Tile backsplash:** $500 to $1,500 installed.
- **Templating:** $50 to $150, often credited if you buy, per Slabwise.
- **Cabinet reinforcement:** a few hundred dollars when heavy stone needs it.
- **Removing the old tops:** ask whether it's on the quote. Sometimes it isn't.
- **Reconnecting the sink, faucet and dishwasher:** fabricators usually don't do it. That's a job for a licensed plumber. We coordinate it so you don't have to.

**Example:** 40 square feet of tier 1 granite with an eased edge, one undermount sink cutout and a tile backsplash comes to about $1,600 to $3,200 for the stone, $100 to $200 for the cutout and $500 to $1,500 for the backsplash. That's roughly **$2,200 to $4,900** before the reconnection.

For a reality check, a North Carolina homeowner shared quotes on Reddit of $3,800 to $4,300 for 45 square feet of 3 cm granite with a half bullnose edge and an undermount sink, and a Raleigh commenter called that normal. Real quotes often land in the upper half of national ranges.

## 8 ways to get affordable countertops without cutting quality

![Small white kitchen with light countertops and a gray subway tile backsplash](/images/projects/Ifixx-portfolio11.webp)

1. **Ask for remnants.** Leftover slab pieces run 30% to 70% less than a full slab, according to Slabwise. They fit vanities, small islands and short runs.

   They're rarely advertised, so ask your fabricator or stone yard. A remnant is often enough for the vanity in a [bathroom remodel](/remodeling/bathroom-remodeling).
2. **Pick tier 1 over tier 3.** Tiers price the rarity of the color. As raw material, tier 1 runs $8 to $15 per square foot and tier 3 $25 to $50 per Slabwise, for stone that lasts just as long.
3. **Keep a straight edge.** An eased or square edge usually comes included. Fancy profiles add $10 to $40 per linear foot.
4. **Keep the sink where it is.** Every cutout costs $100 to $200, and moving the sink also means moving the water and drain lines.
5. **Mix materials.** Stone on the perimeter and butcher block on the island, or the other way around.
6. **Tile the backsplash.** A full-height slab backsplash is billed like more countertop, by the square foot. Tile runs $500 to $1,500 installed.
7. **Get three itemized quotes.** Material, fabrication, cutouts, edge, delivery, installation and removal of the old tops, each on its own line. A 20% spread between quotes for the same stone is normal, per Slabwise.
8. **Skip the premium sealer.** A good penetrating sealer costs $15 to $25 and goes on in about 20 minutes.

If you're going to save somewhere, save on rarity and decoration. The stone underneath is the same.

## Where people overspend (and where they shouldn't save)

Most overspending goes to what you notice on day one: a rarer color, a fancier edge, a slab backsplash. Most regret comes from what you can't see: what holds the stone up and who connects it. This is the part we don't cut corners on.

- **3 cm thickness in the kitchen.** A 2 cm slab saves only $200 to $500 on a whole kitchen and needs a plywood base underneath, per Slabwise. Not worth it.
- **Approving the seam layout before anyone cuts.** Ask to see where every seam falls on the slab. Once it's cut, it's cut.
- **Level, solid cabinets.** New stone on cabinets that rock or sit out of level can crack. Heavy stone sometimes needs a few hundred dollars of reinforcement, and that money is well spent.
- **A dishwasher bracket under stone.** You can't screw a dishwasher into granite or quartz, so it needs a side-mount bracket. Some installers won't hook one up without it.
- **The finish in writing.** Polished, honed or leathered, agreed on paper and seen on the full slab, not only on a small sample. Altering a quartz finish after install can void the manufacturer's warranty.
- **A licensed plumber for the reconnection.** The sink, faucet and dishwasher go back in after the stone. We schedule that visit so your kitchen isn't out of service longer than it needs to be.

## From template to install: how long countertop installation takes

The installation itself usually takes one day. The wait is fabrication: 5 to 14 business days from templating to install, per Slabwise. Here's the order that keeps it on schedule:

1. **Cabinets set, level and secured.** Nothing gets measured before this.
2. **Sink, faucet and cooktop chosen.** The fabricator needs them to cut the openings.
3. **Templating.** The countertop is measured on the installed cabinets.
4. **Fabrication.** 5 to 14 business days.
5. **Installation.** One day.
6. **Reconnection, then backsplash.** A licensed plumber reconnects the sink and dishwasher, and the tile backsplash goes on after the tops are in.

If the cabinets run late, every step after them moves.
